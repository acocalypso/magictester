# MTG gameplay data review and a Forge-based improvement foundation

Verified 7 November 2026. The supplied research is useful, with several material corrections. External human data can provide priors, examples and partial evaluations; the dependable source of full decision states and legal alternatives for our project should be a recorder built into Forge. This document is a research/design result, not an implemented ingestion pipeline or trained AI.

The original 17lands page is client rendered. I inspected the JavaScript it serves and the public CMS document used by that page to verify its links and licensing description. That CMS access is not a promised stable developer API. The audit read a small source sample and dataset previews; it did not download multi-gigabyte training archives or crawl a game archive.

## Important schema and quality corrections

17lands does contain combat information. In the raw sample, `creatures_attacked`, `creatures_blocking` and `creatures_blocked` contain pipe-separated Arena card-definition IDs. End-of-turn life and combat-damage fields are also present. These are more informative than just counts, but they do not establish complete instance-level combat state, damage assignment, priority windows or every legal alternative. Repeated copies/tokens, counters, tapped status and effects require more context. Attacker/blocker pairing semantics must be validated, not invented by simply zipping arrays.

The tidy corpus distinguishes whose **turn** a row describes from whose statistic appears inside that row. Player-relative turn numbering must not be confused with one global turn sequence. Do not reject an alleged mana inconsistency using a naive lands-equal-mana rule: alternate costs, free spells and mana effects exist.

A concrete quality problem was found in the first 100 `replay_turns` preview rows returned by the dataset server: three populated `eot_oppo_life` values were non-numeric pipe-separated strings, including `77479|77463`. This is a field/type violation. Another row reported `77463` in that life field on turn 1. The cause—raw source corruption, transformation, or schema drift—was **not established**. This is not a random corpus sample and must not be described as a corpus-wide error rate. Quarantine affected records/partitions until compared with their original source. The first eight correctly unpacked AFR source rows were inspected separately; their first row has valid first-turn life values of 20.

The public `replay_hands` preview contains candidate opening-hand lists, while ordinary game data generally describes the kept hand. Rejected hands plus the eventual result do not reveal the counterfactual result had each rejected hand been kept. Likewise, `on_play` records a circumstance; it is not universally a deliberate play/draw decision. Account for Arena Bo1 hand smoothing and historical opening-hand definitions when evaluating a Forge mulligan policy.

Game-wide drawn/tutored cards and final results are useful labels or summaries, but are future information when constructing an earlier decision's features. GIH win rates are observational, affected by deck context and player selection. They are not direct removal priorities or causal estimates of a card's value in a particular board state.

## DraftZero architecture update

The [current README](https://github.com/danieljbrooks/draft-zero) says development has moved to the Go-based gorge engine; the XMage/MageZero pipeline remains in [v0.1.0](https://github.com/danieljbrooks/draft-zero/releases/tag/v0.1.0). Current author-reported experiments also note earlier opponent-decklist and mana-accounting confounds. Its results should therefore be treated as experiment-specific reports, not evidence of Forge performance or universal MTG strength. The useful ideas are reproducible actor/learner/evaluator runs, typed decisions, legal masks, recorded search targets, fixed opponents and explicit information boundaries.

## Tools and additional sources

- [spells-mtg](https://pypi.org/project/spells-mtg/): live package, version 0.17.0 at review, MIT metadata. Useful ingestion/analysis tooling; not an independent gameplay source. Verify replay coverage in the selected version instead of assuming every 17lands artifact is supported.
- `joelnitta/magicr` redirects to [joelnitta/mtgr](https://github.com/joelnitta/mtgr). Its DESCRIPTION and LICENSE.md identify MIT. The supplied old gists remain analysis examples, not an additional corpus or a definitive schema specification.
- [MTGWeightedSampling](https://github.com/timblewis/MTGWeightedSampling): available analysis script; root contains README and Python source, with no explicit license detected. It works on 17lands data. Do not assume reuse permission from public visibility.
- [MTGA-Tapps](https://github.com/pattont/MTGA-Tapps): active local Arena tracker with SQLite and play-by-play features; repository declares AGPL-3.0. Copying its implementation and parsing a documented export are different licensing questions. Its software license does not automatically determine a user's log license.
- [Cockatrice replay storage](https://github.com/Cockatrice/Cockatrice/blob/master/servatrice/servatrice.ini.example) and community [cube-tools](https://github.com/caseydavenport/cube-tools) show another replay-collection route. Replay availability for participants is not an openly licensed central training archive. Manual tabletop actions also need rules validation before use as legal-action labels.
- [MTGO's official Game History/replay instructions](https://help.mtgo.com/hc/en-us/articles/6046177142811-Finding-your-League-Game-ID-number) establish client replay access, not a general public bulk export/training license.
- Academic Draftsim datasets are chiefly draft-pick data. Kaggle mirrors can duplicate existing sources. No maintained, clearly licensed, comprehensive human Constructed/four-player Commander decision dataset was established by this review. This is a search finding, not proof that none can exist.
- Tournament decklists/results and broadcasts remain useful for deck selection and manually annotated scenarios. A video or results page is not a complete machine-readable game trajectory; a worldwide claim that official paper events have no play records would be too absolute.

## Licensing implications for a Forge project

[CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) permits copying and adaptation under its terms, with appropriate attribution, a license link and indication of changes. Keep a source/version/transformation manifest with derived datasets and model documentation. The public-dump permission and the publisher's separate curated-statistics guidelines must not be conflated.

Forge's GPL covers the relevant program code; it does not automatically relicense an external dataset or settle the license of trained weights. Keep code, data and model licensing explicit. MIT software can be reused with its notices in the GPL project; an AGPL implementation needs deliberate compatibility handling. Check what rights a particular dataset license actually covers, and do not assume it grants card-art, trademark, privacy or unrelated third-party rights. Unlicensed/restricted sources stay out of the training inventory until the required permission is established. No permission request was sent to any source owner during this review.

## Recommended shared foundation on Forge

Build a project-owned Java decision-recording and policy layer around Forge's existing rules engine, shared by Android, desktop and Docker. An initial design can live under `engine-ai/`; this is a proposed layout, not an implementation already created:

```text
engine-ai/
  schema/       versioned observation, decision, action and provenance contracts
  recorder/     Forge hooks before choices and after resolution
  adapters/     external partial observations and card/rules mappings
  scenarios/    curated tactical fixtures and held-out evaluation suites
  training/     off-device data validation, labeling and model experiments
  runtime/      local scorer/search integration and safe heuristic fallback
```

1. **Create a manifest and small audit first.** Pin source revisions, checksums, dates, event/format, declared license and permission status. Detect actual archive contents. Validate field types, card mappings, missing diffs, join keys and duplicate/perspective overlap. Store invalid examples separately. Start with a supported Limited set; do not download all 25 GB of a mirror to discover its schema afterward.
2. **Keep partial observations distinct from full decisions.** A common event format is useful for analytics, but absent fields remain unknown. A turn-summary adapter must not invent action order, hidden cards or legal-action lists. Record completeness and confidence. Only reconstruct a Forge position when the available evidence passes rules and state checks; compare observable patterns otherwise.
3. **Record complete decisions directly in Forge.** At choice boundaries capture actor-visible state, format/rules/AI versions, legal options, selected option, timing and optional search visit/value targets. Record stable instance references and the information model. Factor choices into cast/pass, modes, targets, attacks, blocks and other engine decisions rather than constructing an enormous card-name vocabulary. Keep privileged replay/debug state separate from model input. Recorded choices and random outcomes are needed for robust replay; seeds alone are insufficient for time-bounded search.
4. **Use each source for the task it supports.** Use validated 17lands data for Limited priors, value features and carefully qualified opening-hand/behavior tests. Generate reliable legal-action and search labels in Forge, including Commander, partners, control, combo and BO3 sideboarding. Retain losses and unusual positions; self-play can otherwise reproduce the incumbent's blind spots. Add consented human corrections to curated scenarios.
5. **Train off-device; execute a small policy locally.** Begin with an interpretable card/action scorer or small value model integrated with bounded search. Forge supplies legality. Include format, opponent count and action semantics so 20-life Limited preferences are not blindly applied to multiplayer Commander. Preserve heuristic fallback for unsupported features. A search teacher should use the same fair information boundary as the deployed agent.
6. **Evaluate independently of the training corpus.** Split related drafts/games and mirrored perspectives together, hold out dates/sets/archetypes, and prevent future-state leakage. Measure both move agreement and paired performance against frozen Forge versions, with deck/seat rotations, tactical tests, legality, timeout rates and physical-device latency/memory. Human-vs-human win rates from Arena cannot directly establish an AI's strength against a different opponent population.

This foundation supplies the exact decision data missing from public summaries, while benefiting from Forge's existing card/rules implementation and upstream AI work. It integrates with the earlier [Forge AI improvement plan](../FORGE_AI_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN.md). It does not require replacing the rules engine or adding a cloud dependency to the standalone application.
